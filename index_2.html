Why people fall for West Shore

Lots here are wide, streets end at the water, and no two blocks look alike — original 1960s bungalows and sidesplits, renovated two-storeys, and custom rebuilds taking in bay and lake views. Homes range accordingly, which is exactly why a neighbourhood-level comparison matters more here than almost anywhere in Pickering. We live and work in Pickering — ask us what a street-by-street difference is worth.

The bay life

Frenchman's Bay Yacht Club, the Pickering Rouge Canoe Club and dragonboat crews all call the bay home — plus an accessible kayak launch at the Rotary park.

Beaches both directions

A sandy beach on the bay's west spit, and Rouge Beach at the national park's edge — both an easy bike ride on the Waterfront Trail.

Petticoat Creek on the doorstep

A full TRCA conservation park inside the neighbourhood's west edge — pool and splash pad in season, bluff-top lookouts year-round.

Schools you can walk to

Three elementary schools sit inside the neighbourhood — public, Catholic, and the area's French Immersion school.

Room to grow

Wide lots from the cottage-country era make West Shore one of Pickering's favourite streets for additions, rebuilds and custom infill.

Commuter's corner

Whites Road's 401 interchange is at the top of the neighbourhood, Pickering GO about five minutes east — downtown Toronto in roughly half an hour.
